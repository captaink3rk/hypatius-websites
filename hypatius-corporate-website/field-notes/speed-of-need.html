Moving at the speed of need

The Space Force rebuilt how it buys. Industry now has to rebuild how it decides.

Short answer. The Space Force has reorganized acquisition around speed. Small, non-traditional teams can match that pace if they treat Human-AI Teaming as a discipline: AI does the analysis, people own the intent and the commit, and every decision is recorded. That is what we will talk about at Space Industry Days this week.

This week in Los Angeles

AFCEA Los Angeles opens Space Industry Days tomorrow at the LAX Marriott. The theme is "Fielding the Arsenal of Freedom – Victory Through Industry Partnership." Over two days, the Space Force portfolio leads for missile warning and tracking, BMC3I, space-based sensing and targeting, space combat power, satcom and PNT, and space access will brief industry. A small-business track runs both days on prime partnerships and on how to grow into a strategic space industry partner. HYPATIUS will be in the room.

The government moved first

The Space Force has reorganized its acquisition enterprise into portfolios. Each portfolio acquisition executive can trade inside a mission area, not program by program [SpaceNews; ExecutiveGov]. In September, the Department of the Air Force stood up a Technology Portfolio Executive to focus on the "valley of death" between good technology and operational use [DefenseScoop, Sept. 15]. The Chief of Space Operations has told every Guardian to train the way they will fight and to decide at all levels through Mission Command [CSO C-Note #3-2, Oct. 13].

The Air Force is moving the same way. Kessel Run, its first software factory, is now government-led and vendor-managed. Its new senior materiel leader treats pipelines and cloud as commodities and buys mission capability instead [Air & Space Forces Magazine, Oct. 16].

That is a buyer asking for speed. The open question is whether industry can supply it.

Speed of need is a decision problem

Most of what slows a program is not engineering. It is the time between knowing something and deciding what to do about it: analysis that has to be run again, options that nobody priced, approvals that nobody can trace. Faster paperwork does not fix that. Faster, better-grounded decisions do.

That is where Human-AI Teaming earns its name. In a good team, the machine does what machines do well: it correlates sensor data, propagates orbits, prices courses of action and looks ahead. The person does what only a person should do: set the intent, weigh the risk and commit. The record captures who decided what and why, so the next person does not start from zero.

Defining the art of the possible

We built STARCHITECT, our space wargaming and BMC3I platform, around that loop. An operator states intent. The system lays out courses of action, each with its cost, a pass/fail verdict and a look-ahead. A named operator commits, and every operator commit is recorded.

STARCHITECT does not stop at the physics. It runs on Orekit-based orbital physics and spans 63 modules across 273 APIs [starchitect.us/capabilities]. Then it turns that physics into insight an operator can act on, so a team can fight and win in space at the speed of advantage. It is designed to keep working through DDIL (denied, disrupted, intermittent, and limited) conditions, because the moment you most need a decision is usually the moment the link is worst.

It is also built for partnership, which is this week's theme. FMI/FMU sandbox vaulting lets a prime inject its own physics as compiled binaries, without exposing its IP [starchitect.us/capabilities]. A small team brings the wargame. A prime brings its models. Neither gives up what it owns.

A small team built this. That is not a boast about headcount. It shows that a small, non-traditional team that uses AI with discipline can deliver at the pace the new acquisition system asks for.

What I am asking of the room

To program offices: give small teams a scenario, not a requirements document, and judge us on the run. To primes: bring us your hardest mission thread and your own models. To other small businesses: partner with us. No single company will field the arsenal.

The same discipline, off the ops floor

We use the same rule on the business side. ALIDADE, our capture intelligence platform, is designed to help small firms decide which opportunities deserve their hours. CRATON is designed to keep their operations audit-ready once they win. Both are in development for 2027.

If you are at Space Industry Days, find us. If you are not, request a briefing at hypati.us or see STARCHITECT at starchitect.us.
